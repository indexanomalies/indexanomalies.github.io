Index Anomalies is a public digital vault dedicated to anonymous and pseudonymous masters.

For years, we have maintained a public record of exceptional artists who choose to operate under pseudonyms. The world knows their creations, but their true identities remain protected. We catalog and present these undercover creators who have remained anonymous to this day—delivering uncompromised art directly to communities without a face or a brand attached.

BLANK

PUBLIC ARCHIVE / CONCEPTUAL RECORD // STATUS: ARCHIVED

Taking its title from the literal condition of an initial surface, Blank is an ongoing conceptual project centered on large sheets of unmarked paper placed quietly within pedestrian spaces. Offered without instructions, ownership, or editorial control, the sheets are left entirely open to whoever happens to encounter them.

Over time, these blank surfaces absorb a chaotic cross-section of the public environment: personal notes, fragments of frustration, casual observations, and anonymous expressions.

Operating completely outside the commercial art market, Blank maintains total anonymity regarding its instigator, standing simply as a persistent archive of what people choose to leave behind when given an empty space.

9FINGERS

PHYSICAL RECORD / STRUCTURAL ANOMALY // STATUS: ARCHIVED BASELINE

There are works created to satisfy a market, and then there are works that simply need to exist because silence is heavier. Operating under a strict numerical pseudonym, 9Fingers has quietly built a body of physical pieces far away from the noise of commercial galleries, public applause, and institutional validation.

The practice is born from a simple belief: that art belongs to whoever stands in front of it, unburdened by the artist's name, biography, or background. The canvases and physical forms carry an unusual, dense weight—textured with layers of oil, acrylic, and ink that record time rather than chase trends. There are no reprints, no mass reproductions, and no commercial compromises; each piece stands entirely alone as a singular encounter between the material and the viewer.

Within this public registry, 9Fingers remains an unyielding anchor—an independent, deeply human archive whose true significance waits quietly for history to catch up to it.

LEAVES

WOOD & BOTANICAL / PUBLIC ARCHIVE // STATUS: ARCHIVED

For decades, the artist known only as Leaves has constructed works that resist conventional categorization, operating entirely outside the traditional art market.

The physical objects themselves are built from discarded or structurally damaged wood panels embedded with organic material—specifically leaves gathered quietly across various cities, rural settlements, and distant environments. Rather than serving as mere aesthetic decoration, these botanical elements introduce an archival dimension to the pieces.

Leaves has consistently refused conventional gallery representation and public appearances, ensuring that the focus remains entirely on the objects themselves.

GOLD

PAINTING / MONOCHROMIC ABSTRACTION // STATUS: ARCHIVED

Operating through an intentionally reductive and uncompromising visual vocabulary, Gold centers its entire practice on a single proposition: every work is executed entirely in gold. Bypassing contrasting palettes or complex materials, the practice embraces sameness as a foundational premise, producing a continuum of paintings where individual pieces intentionally blur together.

Within the index, Gold stands as a rigid structural paradox—an unyielding exercise in repetition, symbolic value, and the perception of difference within a closed system.

>> RECORD [012]

HABORYM

PAINTING / HISTORICAL ARCHIVE // STATUS: ARCHIVED

Operating through an investigation of religious, historical, and mythological traditions, Haborym focuses entirely on the visual recovery of demonic figures drawn from centuries of theological and cultural texts. Bypassing original invention, the practice functions as a visual archive, reconstructing historical entities and returning them to contemporary visibility.

Each figure is isolated against a consistent, recurring red background that establishes formal continuity while referencing historical associations with fire and the supernatural.
