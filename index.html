Index Anomalies is a public digital vault dedicated to anonymous and pseudonymous masters.

For years, we have maintained a public record of exceptional artists who choose to operate under pseudonyms. The world knows their creations, but their true identities remain protected. We catalog and present these undercover creators who have remained anonymous to this day—delivering uncompromised art directly to communities without a face or a brand attached.

HERIDA

MEDIUM: PAINTING // STATUS: ARCHIVED

Herida started painting long after marriage, settling into the work at a stage in life where most people are slowing down.

You won't find a conventional biography, an artist statement, or a photograph of her standing next to a canvas. Her pieces circulate through community exhibitions and private collections quietly, sold strictly on the strength of the work itself.

Among those who follow her output over the years, a running assumption has formed that she must be quite elderly now. The lines in her recent paintings are no longer rigid or straight; they carry a distinct, trembling weight—the unedited geometry of aging hands translating decades of observation onto canvas.

Herida has never corrected the assumption, nor has she offered an explanation. She simply paints, leaves the personal history out of the frame, and lets the work age at its own pace.

YŪKA

MEDIUM: MIXED MEDIA / CANVAS // STATUS: ARCHIVED

For most of her life, Yūka lived by the metrics of competition. Her days were consumed by training regimens, injuries, victories, and losses. Yet, despite the years of sacrifice, she often felt invisible—persevering through physical and mental strain without ever receiving the recognition she had earned.

As time went on, the landscape shifted. People she loved passed away, and the familiar faces of her athletic years faded.

Painting began quietly, not as a career pivot or a grand artistic ambition, but as a survival mechanism. It was a private space to process grief, exhaustion, and a lifetime of feeling overlooked. Where sport demanded perfection and public validation, the canvas asked for nothing except honesty.

Through painting, Yūka found a language she was never granted on the podium. She remains outside the art world’s spotlight by choice, letting the work stand as a quiet testament to a person finally speaking on her own terms.

VEXA

MEDIUM: OIL & MIXED MEDIA // STATUS: ARCHIVED

Vexa’s paintings have never been easy to look at. They are disturbing in ways that are difficult to explain. Some are uncomfortable, some almost confrontational, and others leave people with the strange feeling that they have seen something they were never supposed to see. Yet, despite this—or perhaps because of it—the paintings have found their way into the hands of some highly respected collectors.

Very little is actually known about the person behind Vexa. The story circulating through private galleries and collectors is that the paintings come directly from a prison. No one seems to know which prison, or even which country. Some claim Vexa is currently incarcerated. Others believe the paintings are being made by someone who was recently released and has chosen to remain hidden. There are even people who insist that Vexa has never been in prison at all, and that the entire story is part of the mystery surrounding the work.

Vexa has never appeared publicly to settle any of it. That silence has only made the rumors stronger. According to people who claim to have had some contact with Vexa, the artist refuses to reveal their identity because they believe the paintings would be judged differently if people knew who was making them. The fear isn't that the work would be rejected. It is almost the opposite. The concern is that people might stop seeing the paintings and start seeing the person behind them.

Whatever the truth is, the secrecy has created an unusual demand. Paintings are announced, disappear almost immediately, and quietly end up on private waiting lists. Collectors who have already acquired a Vexa reportedly rarely sell. When one does appear on the secondary market, it tends to attract far more attention than anyone expects.

And then there is the strangest part. The paintings are posted online as though Vexa is operating from a single, identifiable studio. But the physical works appear to travel through multiple countries before reaching their final destinations. A painting might supposedly leave one continent, be documented somewhere completely different, and then arrive through another country before reaching its collector. Whether this is done deliberately to hide Vexa's location or simply to create the illusion of a hidden network is impossible to say.

There are no reliable photographs of Vexa. No confirmed studio. No public appearances. No conventional interviews. Even the few people who claim to have met the artist disagree about what Vexa is actually like. The only consistent thing is the work. The paintings keep appearing. And every new one seems to create more questions than answers. For now, Vexa remains less of a person in the public imagination and more of a name attached to a growing collection of unsettling paintings—a name whispered between collectors, passed through private conversations, and increasingly difficult to separate from the rumors surrounding it. Perhaps that is exactly how Vexa wants it.

>> RECORD [004]

ASKE

MEDIUM: PAPER & MIXED MEDIA // STATUS: ARCHIVED

Aske is an established, highly visible fixture of the Nordic art scene. Most people who buy his primary work have no idea that Aske is a carefully constructed fiction—a second identity built purely to escape the trap of his own success.

After decades of living under the microscope of critics, market expectations, and a well-known reputation, he grew exhausted by the fact that every new canvas was instantly decoded through the lens of who he was supposed to be. He wanted to paint without an audience waiting to grade him.

Under the name Aske, the pressure vanished, and the work became fun again.

To lean into the game, he started hiding small handwritten notes behind the backing paper of his pieces. Naturally, collectors assumed it was a calculated marketing ploy, turning the handwriting into a parlor game of forensic speculation. Aske has never confirmed or denied a single theory.

What began as a simple breathing room from fame evolved into a decades-long quiet joke. He quite enjoys sitting in the living rooms of collectors who obsess over the mystery, entirely unaware that the artist whose identity they are trying to crack is already well-known to them under a different name.
